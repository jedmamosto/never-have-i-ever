## Executive Summary

When Western party games are imported into Philippine social gatherings without cultural translation, they fail decisively. Prompts referencing *frat houses, tailgate parties, Venmo requests, Thanksgiving dinners, Irish exits, and high school proms* create immediate cognitive friction. Filipino players either stare blankly, debate the definition of terms, or detach emotionally because the scenarios fail to mirror their lived social reality.

To resonate with modern Filipino players—spanning university *barkadas* (DLSU, UST, Ateneo, UP, UST, FEU), young corporate professionals (BGC, Makati, Ortigas), and neighborhood *inuman* circles—a prompt must tap into authentic Philippine social tensions:
1. **The Tension of Pakikisama vs. Self-Preservation**: E.g., dodging the *ambag* (shared bill), passing on the *tagay* (shared drinking glass), or sneaking out of an *inuman* via "ninja moves".
2. **The Tension of Hiya vs. Marites Curiosity**: E.g., lurking on someone's Instagram dump account, creating a secret group chat without one member, or eavesdropping on condo elevator drama.
3. **The Relatable Anti-Hero (*Marupok* & *Katamaran*)**: E.g., replying within 0.5 seconds to a toxic ex, staying in an undefined 6-month "talking stage", or texting *"on the way na!"* while still wrapped in a towel in bed.

### Crucial Language Calibration: Natural Philippine English + Organic Taglish
As clarified by the user:
> **"I meant by Filipino version like relatable to them. Not necessarily tagalog... Although maybe we can mix it up with taglish I guess."**

Modern urban Filipinos do **not** play drinking games in archaic, formal Tagalog (*"Kailanman ay hindi pa ako nakaranas..."* sounds like a 1970s radio soap opera). Nor do they speak in foreign American vernacular. They speak in **natural Philippine English seamlessly blended with iconic colloquial Taglish terms** (*ambag, inuman, tagay, pulutan, marupok, budol, tita, samgyup, sawsawan, ninja moves, situationship, soft launch*). 

Every prompt in this framework strictly adheres to the core mechanical gates:
- **15 to 68 characters**
- **3 to 12 words**
- **Verb-first past-participle syntax**
- **Capital letter start, terminal punctuation**
- **Zero "Never have I ever" prefix**

## 2. Sociolinguistic Architecture & Tonal Register

### 2.1 The Code-Switching Sweet Spot
Filipino social conversation sits on a continuum between formal English and colloquial Tagalog. For mobile social games, the ideal register is **Colloquial Urban Philippine English (CUPE)** seasoned with iconic Taglish vocabulary:

```
[Archaic Pure Tagalog] ◄────────────── [CUPE + Taglish Sweet Spot] ──────────────► [Sterile Western English]
"Kailanman ay hindi pa                     "Dodged the inuman ambag                      "Skipped the frat party
ako nakapagsinungaling..."                  by pretending to sleep."                      cover charge by lying..."
(Too formal, theatrical)                   (Organic, energetic, authentic)               (Alien, un-relatable)
```

### 2.2 Linguistic Rules for Prompt Formulation

1. **Past-Participle Verb Anchors**:
   Every prompt must complete the unspoken thought: *"[Never have I ever...] {Action}"*.
   - Allowed: `Dodged...`, `Claimed...`, `Beltered...`, `Cried...`, `Soft-launched...`, `Stayed...`, `Booked...`, `Lost...`
   - Disallowed: `I have dodged...`, `Never did I...`, `Do you ever...`

2. **Taglish Morphological Integration**:
   - Pair standard English verbs with recognized Filipino cultural nouns:
     - `Dodged the inuman ambag by playing asleep.` (English verb + Tagalog social ritual)
     - `Burned my tongue on a Peach Mango Pie.` (English verb + Pinoy cultural food)
     - `Hid leftover meat under lettuce at samgyup.` (English verb + Philippine dining quirk)
   - When using Pinoy slang terms as verbs, use standard English past-tense inflections where common:
     - `Soft-launched a situationship on my IG story.`
     - `Beltered an Aegis song with full emotion.`

3. **Strict Mechanical Gate Enforcement**:
   - **Character Length**: `15 <= length <= 68` characters.
   - **Word Count**: `3 <= count <= 12` words.
   - **Punctuation**: Capital letter first; terminal period (`.`) or question mark (`?`).
   - **No Artificial Fillers**: Eliminate filler clauses (*"just because"*, *"in order to"*, *"purely so that"*).

## 4. Cultural Boundaries & Negative Disqualifiers in Philippine Social Play

In Philippine society, maintaining **psychological safety** requires strict adherence to cultural taboos. Certain topics are *malignant violations* under Benign Violation Theory (McGraw & Warren, 2010), causing discomfort, defensive silence, or real social harm.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE PHILIPPINE GAMEPLAY SAFETY GATE                     │
├──────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│    STRICTLY BANNED (MALIGNANT)       │      APPROVED & ENCOURAGED (BENIGN)     │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ❌ Severe Poverty Mockery            │ ✔️ Relatable Kuripot Quirks             │
│    (Slums, extreme deprivation)      │    (Shopee voucher hunting, free rice)  │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ❌ Partisan Politics & Red-Tagging   │ ✔️ Universal Bureaucratic Struggles     │
│    (Candidate wars, political trauma)│    (Passport queues, LTO line woes)     │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ❌ Sacred Religious Sacrilege        │ ✔️ Light Cultural Church Customs        │
│    (Blaspheming sacraments, statues) │    (Simbang Gabi bibingka, long homily) │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ❌ Harassment & Coercive Dating      │ ✔️ Playful Harot & Romantic Banter      │
│    (Non-consensual acts, doxxing)    │    (Soft launch, secret crush, marupok) │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.1 Four Negative Disqualifiers

1. **Severe Poverty Exploitation / Classist Mockery**:
   - *Why*: The Philippines possesses profound socioeconomic divides. Prompts mocking squatter settlements, begging, hunger, or mistreating household help (*kasambahay*) are deeply repulsive and punch down.
   - *Boundary Line*: Kuripot behavior (*"Haggled at Divisoria"*, *"Dodged the delivery fee"*) is hilarious; poverty mockery is toxic.

2. **Toxic Political Red-Tagging & Partisan Vitriol**:
   - *Why*: Philippine electoral politics is intensely tribal and emotionally volatile. Prompts referencing political candidates, martial law trauma, or red-tagging instantly ruin party momentum and spark bitter ideological arguments.
   - *Boundary Line*: Universal civic friction (*"Fell asleep in a government agency queue"*) is acceptable; partisan attacks are strictly banned.

3. **Offensive Religious Sacrilege**:
   - *Why*: The Philippines remains predominantly Catholic and Christian, with substantial Muslim populations in Mindanao. Directly insulting holy communion, religious icons, or sacred sacraments creates acute moral offense.
   - *Boundary Line*: Harmless cultural rituals (*"Attended Simbang Gabi purely for bibingka and puto bumbong"*, *"Fell asleep during a Sunday homily"*) are benign and warmly embraced.

4. **Malicious Harassment, Extortion & Non-Consensual Acts**:
   - *Why*: Non-consensual touch, predatory stalking, blackmailing someone with private photos, or date spiking are serious crimes, not playful party prompts.
   - *Boundary Line*: All romantic and spicy prompts must reflect **enthusiastic, consensual adult interactions** (*flirting, situationships, mutual making out, sneaky consensual flings*).

### 4.2 The Six Lovable Pinoy Anti-Hero Archetypes

Rather than portraying perfect, polished individuals, great Philippine prompts celebrate the **lovable anti-hero**—the flaws, shortcuts, and human weaknesses that every Filipino secretly recognizes in themselves and their friends:

1. **The *Marupok* (Emotionally Fragile Romantic)**:
   - Zero romantic self-control; easily swayed by the bare minimum attention; returns to red-flag exes within 48 hours of crying about them.
   - *Archetypal Action*: Replying instantly to a toxic text, falling in love during a 15-minute Angkas ride.

2. **The *Marites* (Communal Intelligence Operative)**:
   - Driven by insatiable curiosity; knows everyone's relationship timeline, dump accounts, and breakup reasons before the people involved even know.
   - *Archetypal Action*: Stalking an ex's new partner's tagged photos from 2018; maintaining secondary group chats.

3. **The *Kuripot / Ambag-Dodger* (Tactical Frugality Specialist)**:
   - Generous in spirit, yet magically disappears or experiences GCash "signal issues" the exact moment the ₱8,000 bar bill arrives on the table.
   - *Archetypal Action*: Rounding down their ambag share; pretending to be asleep in the Grab ride.

4. **The *Katamaran* (Master of Procrastination & Comfort)**:
   - Will go to extreme lengths to avoid minor physical exertion; lies shamelessly about their physical location to stay in bed longer.
   - *Archetypal Action*: Texting *"on the way na"* while still wearing a bathrobe watching Netflix.

5. **The *Harot* (Playful Flirt)**:
   - Radiates flirtatious energy; enjoys casual romantic tension, sneaky hand-holding, and late-night situationship banter.

## 5. Quantitative Verification & Corpus Scaling Checklist

When generating or auditing prompts for the Philippine Never Have I Ever corpus, every card must pass this 7-point mechanical and cultural test:

- [ ] **Gate 1: Character Budget**: Is `text.length` between 15 and 68 characters?
- [ ] **Gate 2: Word Budget**: Is `wordCount` between 3 and 12 words?
- [ ] **Gate 3: Syntactic Form**: Does it start with a capitalized past-participle verb (`Dodged`, `Claimed`, `Cried`, `Lost`)?
- [ ] **Gate 4: Terminal Punctuation**: Does it end with a single period (`.`) or question mark (`?`)?
- [ ] **Gate 5: Clean Content**: Is it free of `"Never have I ever"`, `"I have"`, or leading/trailing quotation marks?
- [ ] **Gate 6: Cultural Resonance**: Does it reference an authentic Philippine situational touchstone or dynamic rather than an imported Western cliché?
- [ ] **Gate 7: Safe Boundary**: Is it free of poverty classism, political red-tagging, religious sacrilege, and malicious harassment?

### Automated Verification Script (`verify-prompt.js`)
```javascript
function validatePinoyPrompt(text) {
  const issues = [];
  const chars = text.length;
  const words = text.trim().split(/\s+/).length;
  
  if (chars < 15 || chars > 68) issues.push(`Char count ${chars} outside [15, 68]`);
  if (words < 3 || words > 12) issues.push(`Word count ${words} outside [3, 12]`);
  if (!/^[A-Z]/.test(text)) issues.push('Must start with capital letter');
  if (!/[.?]$/.test(text)) issues.push('Must end with terminal punctuation (. or ?)');
  if (/^never have i ever/i.test(text)) issues.push('Must NOT start with "Never have I ever"');
  
  // Western cliché blacklist check
  const westernClichés = [/venmo/i, /uber/i, /tailgate/i, /kegger/i, /frat\s*house/i, /irish\s*exit/i, /prom\s*queen/i];
  westernClichés.forEach(regex => {
    if (regex.test(text)) issues.push(`Contains banned Western cliché: ${regex}`);
  });

  return { isValid: issues.length === 0, issues };
}
```
