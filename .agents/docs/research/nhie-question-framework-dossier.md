## Executive Summary

A game of *Never Have I Ever* (NHIE) is not a trivia contest, nor is it a stand-up comedy performance. Mechanically and sociologically, it is an **interpersonal trust engine disguised as a light-hearted drinking or elimination game**. 

When a prompt fails, it fails for one of two diametrically opposed reasons:
1. **The Abstract Cliché Trap**: The prompt is so generic and sanitized (*"Broken a bone"*, *"Been on a plane"*, *"Lied to my parents"*) that compliance is purely transactional. It evokes zero emotional reaction, zero narrative intrigue, and zero conversational follow-up.
2. **The Over-Specified Monologue Trap**: The prompt is written like a comedic tweet (*"Bought an expensive bag of artisanal organic spinach purely to watch it liquefy in the crisper drawer over three weeks"*). This pre-chews the punchline, inflates reading latency, and collapses the player confession rate toward zero because players nitpick whether their experience matches the hyper-specific scenario.

The **Golden Maxim of Social Game Design** established in this research dossier is:
> **"The card is the match; the players are the fireworks."**
> A high-impact prompt provides a lean, concrete situational anchor (3 to 12 words, 15 to 68 characters) that triggers immediate recognition, establishes psychological safety through relatable vulnerability, and demands an immediate follow-up question from the room: *"Wait, who drank? What happened?!"*

This dossier delivers the complete empirical, theoretical, and operational framework required to generate and validate 400 new questions (40 per level across Levels 1 to 10), ensuring unwavering mechanical consistency, optimal social entropy, and flawless escalation across all three tiers (**Chill**, **Party**, and **Spicy**).

### 1.1 The Psychological Engine: Why People Play Never Have I Ever

To engineer questions that reliably produce group engagement, we must ground our design in four foundational behavioral theories:

#### A. Social Penetration Theory & The Onion Model (Altman & Taylor, 1973)
Altman and Taylor demonstrated that interpersonal relationship development moves systematically from superficial, outer layers (demographic facts, everyday habits) to central, vulnerable layers (private transgressions, romantic secrets, core insecurities). 
* **The Breadth-Depth Vector**: Early interaction requires *breadth* (exploring varied low-stakes topics) before players permit *depth* (intimate self-disclosure).
* **The Reciprocal Vulnerability Loop**: Self-disclosure is governed by the norm of reciprocity (Gouldner, 1960). In ordinary conversation, disclosing a vulnerable truth carries unilateral risk. In NHIE, however, the prompt forces *simultaneous* physical disclosure (taking a drink or dropping a finger). Because multiple players drink at the exact same millisecond, social exposure is collectivized. The risk of rejection is neutralized.
* **Depenetration Hazard**: If a game forces Stage 3/4 disclosures (deep romantic or taboo lore) before establishing Stage 1/2 safety, players experience acute threat, triggering **depenetration**—social withdrawal, defensive lying, or nervous silence.

#### B. Benign Violation Theory (McGraw & Warren, 2010; McGraw et al., 2012)
Peter McGraw and Caleb Warren established that humor and playful amusement occur if and only if three conditions are satisfied simultaneously:
1. **A norm violation occurs**: An ethical, social, physical, or linguistic boundary is breached.
2. **The violation is appraised as benign**: The player perceives the breach as safe, inconsequential, and harmless.
3. **Simultaneous appraisal**: Both evaluations happen concurrently.

Applied to NHIE prompts:
* **Failure Mode A: Violation = Absent (Boredom)**. Prompts like *"Drank coffee this morning"* or *"Owned a bicycle"* present zero violation. The situation is completely normal. Outcome: Total flatline.
* **Failure Mode B: Violation = Malignant (Offense/Defense)**. Prompts like *"Stolen money from a close friend's purse"* or *"Cheated on a spouse of ten years"* represent non-benign, severe moral transgressions. They evoke disgust, judgment, or awkward defensiveness.
* **The Sweet Spot: Benign Violations (Laughter & Connection)**. Prompts like *"Blamed bad Wi-Fi just to leave a video call"* or *"Sent a screenshot to the person I was screenshotting"* represent clear social violations (dishonesty, pettiness, clumsiness), yet they are universal, low-stakes, and completely non-fatal. Players laugh because the violation is undeniable yet entirely forgivable.

#### C. Pluralistic Ignorance Disruption (Prentice & Miller, 1993; Katz & Allport, 1931)
Pluralistic ignorance is the psychological state where individuals privately harbor an impulse or habit but falsely assume everyone else is more disciplined, virtuous, or "normal." 
When an NHIE card reveals a secret micro-foible (e.g., *"Drafted a text in my Notes app before sending it"* or *"Practiced winning an argument in the shower"*), and 4 out of 6 players drink simultaneously, the room experiences an immediate **cathartic normalization cascade**. Taboos evaporate, collective relief emerges, and social cohesion surges.

#### D. The "Story Catalyst" Mechanism (Loewenstein's Information Gap Theory, 1994)
A great NHIE question is never an end in itself; it is an **incomplete narrative fragment**. George Loewenstein's Information Gap Theory posits that curiosity arises when there is a perceived gap between what one knows and what one wants to know. 
When a player drinks to *"Used a fake name at a bar"* or *"Booked a last-minute trip on a total impulse"*, the physical act of drinking confirms the *existence* of a story while keeping the *details* concealed. This information gap exerts irresistible social pressure on the rest of the group to ask: *"Wait, why did you have to use a fake name? Who were you talking to?!"*

### 1.2 The "Goldilocks Hit Rate" (20% to 70%) & Information Entropy

Why do both universal questions and hyper-niche questions fail catastrophically in live party environments? We can prove this mathematically using Claude Shannon’s Information Entropy (Shannon, 1948).

```
   SHANNON INFORMATION ENTROPY H(p) vs. HIT RATE (p)
   
   H(p) [Bits]
     1.0 ┼                   ╭─────────╮   <-- MAXIMUM SOCIAL SURPRISE (p = 0.50)
         │                ╭──           ──╮
     0.8 ┼              ╭─                 ─╮
         │            ╭─                     ─╮   GOLDILOCKS SWEET SPOT (20% to 70%)
     0.6 ┼           ╭                         ╮  Optimal conversational variance
         │          ╭                           ╮
     0.4 ┼         ╭                             ╮
         │        ╭                               ╮
     0.2 ┼       ╭                                 ╮
         │      ╭                                   ╮
     0.0 ┼──────┴───────────────────────────────────┴──────► Hit Rate p
        0.0 (Nobody drinks)    0.50 (Half drink)    1.0 (Everyone drinks)
        DEAD AIR / ZERO INFO                        ROTE COMPLIANCE / ZERO INFO
```

#### The Mathematical Formulation
Let a party group consist of $N$ players. For any given prompt $Q$, let $p$ represent the probability that an arbitrary player has performed the action (the "Hit Rate"). The binary disclosure event can be modeled as a Bernoulli random variable with Shannon Entropy:
$$H(p) = -p \log_2(p) - (1-p) \log_2(1-p)$$

#### The Two Failure Extremes:
1. **The Universal Extreme ($p \to 1.0$)**:
   - $H(1.0) = 0$ bits of information.
   - If a prompt is universal (*"Checked my phone while eating alone"* or *"Eaten cold pizza"*), every single player drinks. Because everyone drinks, no player is differentiated. There is no minority to interrogate, no majority to banter with, and zero narrative curiosity. Drinking degenerates into an uninspired mechanical routine.
2. **The Hyper-Niche Extreme ($p \to 0.0$)**:
   - $H(0.0) = 0$ bits of information.
   - If a prompt is excessively narrow (*"Lost my shoes at a polo match in Argentina"* or *"Eaten cereal with orange juice out of an ashtray"*), nobody drinks. The prompt lands with a dull thud. The round produces awkward silence, and players often turn their energy toward criticizing the ridiculousness of the card.

#### The 20% to 70% Sweet Spot:
Maximum information entropy occurs at $p = 0.50$ ($H = 1.0$ bit), where uncertainty and surprise are mathematically maximized. 
In practical party group sizes ($N = 4$ to $8$ players):
* **At 20%–35% Hit Rate (1–3 players drink)**: A distinctive minority confesses. The non-drinkers immediately spotlight the drinkers. The drinkers form a conspiratorial bond and are pressured to tell the story.
* **At 50% Hit Rate (Half the room drinks)**: A clean social split. An immediate debate erupts between the "guilty" half and the "innocent" half.
* **At 60%–70% Hit Rate (Majority drinks)**: A playful reversal occurs. The 1 or 2 players who *didn't* drink become the subject of comedic scrutiny (*"Wait, how have you lived 25 years and never done that?!"*).

### 1.3 Escalation Curves & Emotional Safety

#### The Magic Circle & Plausible Deniability (Huizinga, 1938; Salen & Zimmerman, 2004)
Johan Huizinga defined the "Magic Circle" as the temporary, bounded play-space where ordinary social rules and real-world consequences are suspended. Within this circle, admitting to a petty or spicy behavior does not damage a player's real-world social standing; rather, it earns status for authenticity and humor. 
Crucially, drinking provides **plausible deniability**: a player can take a casual sip without saying a word, letting the room speculate without being forced into an unwanted deposition if they feel unready.

#### Amy Edmondson’s Psychological Safety in Group Play (Edmondson, 1999)
For players to take interpersonal risks in the **Party** (L4–L7) and **Spicy** (L8–L10) tiers, the group must first establish a climate of psychological safety—a shared belief that the group will not embarrass, reject, or punish someone for speaking up.
* **The "Warm-Up Gradient"**: Trust is earned sequentially. Level 1–3 prompts establish that confessions will be met with communal laughter and empathy rather than moralistic judgment. 
* Once players see their peers cheerfully confessing to petty fibs and funny quirks, the group’s "psychological immune system" strengthens, paving the way for higher-voltage romantic and social truths.

## 2. The Anatomy of a High-Impact Prompt

### 2.1 Concrete Situational Anchors vs. Abstract Generalizations

Allan Paivio’s Dual-Coding Theory (Paivio, 1971; 1986) proves that human cognition processes concrete language through two independent mental subsystems: an auditory/verbal system and a visual/imaginal system. Abstract concepts activate only the verbal system, while concrete words evoke immediate sensory imagery.

```
       ABSTRACT PROMPT                                CONCRETE ANCHOR
 "Lied about being late."               "Texted 'On my way!' while still in bed."
        │                                                  │
        ▼                                                  ▼
 Verbal code only                                   Dual-coded (Verbal + Sensory)
 Low emotional salience                             Evokes vivid physical imagery:
 Slow recognition latency                           Phone in hand, tangled covers,
 Flat, clinical, forgettable                        visceral memory of lazy panic.
```

When a prompt uses a **Concrete Situational Anchor**, it names a specific physical action, digital artifact, or physical environment. The player does not have to logically evaluate an abstract moral category; their episodic memory flashes instantly to a specific scene.

### 2.2 Micro-Vulnerability & The "Anti-Hero" vs. "Villain" Boundary

A successful NHIE prompt allows the confessor to inhabit the role of the **lovable Anti-Hero**, never the **despicable Villain**.

```
                           THE MORAL VULNERABILITY SPECTRUM
                           
    [BENIGN FOIBLE]             ◄── THE SWEET SPOT ──►             [MALIGNANT ACT]
    "Drank water"            "Left an item at their place"      "Keyed an ex's car"
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►
    Zero Violation           Relatable Human Imperfection        Malignant Violation
    0% Emotion               Empathy, Laughter, Story            Hostility, Shock, Revulsion
    (Boring)                 (THE ANTI-HERO)                     (THE VILLAIN)
```

1. **The Anti-Hero Archetype (Valid & Required)**:
   - Driven by universal human flaws: mild vanity, laziness, petty curiosity, social anxiety, fear of confrontation, or romantic impulsiveness.
   - Examples: *"Faked a phone call to escape an awkward conversation"*, *"Realized mid-argument that I was completely wrong"*, *"Muted someone online instead of unfollowing them"*.
   - **Social Impact**: The table smiles or groans in collective sympathy. Admitting it makes the player seem human, approachable, and fun.
2. **The Villain Archetype (Strictly Disqualified)**:
   - Driven by malice, cruelty, calculated breach of fiduciary/familial trust, or disregard for consent.
   - Examples: *"Intentionally sabotaged a coworker's presentation"*, *"Stole money from family"*, *"Spread a destructive lie about someone's character"*.
   - **Social Impact**: The table goes cold. The player is judged as untrustworthy or unsafe. The Magic Circle shatters.

### 2.3 Syntactic Cadence & The "One-Breath Out-Loud Rule"

In live party settings, cards are read aloud by a designated player over ambient background noise (chatter, music, glasses clinking). The cognitive processing window is razor-thin.

#### The Mechanical Limits
* **Character Budget**: Strictly **15 to 68 characters** (including punctuation).
* **Word Budget**: Strictly **3 to 12 words**.
* **Syntactic Structure**: Verb-first, past participle (*"Pretended..."*, *"Waved..."*, *"Texted..."*, *"Lost..."*). 
* **Terminal Punctuation**: Required (`.`, `!`, `?`).
* **Leading Prefix Ban**: Never include the phrase *"Never have I ever"*. The game UI or the reading player already provides this spoken prefix. Repeating it wastes 18 precious characters and creates stilted phrasing.

#### The 2-Second Noise Gate
A prompt must pass the **2-Second Out-Loud Bar Test**:
* Can the reader articulate the prompt clearly in a single breath without stumbling?
* Does the listener comprehend the entire scenario within 2 seconds of the final word?
* If a prompt requires subordinate clauses (*"while standing in front of someone whom I had known for years purely to avoid..."*), it causes cognitive fatigue. By the time the sentence ends, listeners have forgotten the beginning.

  - Crossing consent boundaries or creating non-consensual sexual pressure.

## 4. Comprehensive Acceptance & Rejection Criteria

To ensure that the 400 new questions maintain the elite quality bar of the seed dataset, every proposed prompt must be evaluated against deterministic positive and negative filters.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                      THE 6-POINT POSITIVE GATE                              |

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                       │
                                       ▼
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     THE 9 NEGATIVE DISQUALIFIERS (STRICT BANS)              |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4.1 Positive Acceptance Criteria (Must-Have Specifications)

1. **Morphological & Syntactic Standard**:
   - Must begin with a capitalized past-participle or past-tense verb phrase (*"Pretended..."*, *"Waved..."*, *"Eaten..."*, *"Kissed..."*, *"Lost..."*, *"Called..."*).
   - Must conclude with terminal punctuation (`.`, `!`, or `?`).
   - Must NEVER contain the literal string `"Never have I ever"`.
2. **Strict Mechanical Envelope**:
   - **Character Length**: `15 <= str.length <= 68` (inclusive of punctuation).
   - **Word Count**: `3 <= words.length <= 12`.
3. **The 2-Second Acoustic Readability Gate**:
   - Must be instantly pronounceable by an average reader over ambient party music in a single breath.
   - Zero complex multi-syllabic jargon (*"anthropological"*, *"counter-intuitive"*, *"existential"*).
4. **Concrete Situational Anchor**:
   - Must reference a real-world object (*"Notes app"*, *"bathroom cabinet"*, *"fridge"*, *"hoodie"*, *"Venmo"*), temporal milestone (*"sunrise"*, *"after midnight"*, *"before lunchtime"*), or physical posture (*"in bed"*, *"in a parked car"*).
5. **Story-Generating Latency < 3 Seconds**:
   - The prompt must create an immediate information gap that provokes peers to ask for context within 3 seconds of card reveal.
6. **Strict Category & Level Mapping**:
   - Must match one of the defined categories assigned to that specific intensity tier:
     - **Chill (L1–L3)**: `SOCIAL FIBS`, `MODERN HABITS`, `CASUAL CHAOS`, `TINY DELUSIONS`, `CULINARY SINS`
     - **Party (L4–L7)**: `PETTY ARCHIVES`, `PARTY LORE`, `TRAVEL CHAOS`, `SOCIAL CRIMES`, `PLOT TWISTS`, `DEEP LORE`
     - **Spicy (L8–L10)**: `DANGEROUS CRUSHES`, `SPICY LORE`, `MIDNIGHT SECRETS`, `ROMANTIC TENSION`, `UNFILTERED CONFESSIONS`

## 5. Generation vs. Borrowing Strategy

A foundational strategic decision for scaling the dataset from 100 to 500 questions is whether to **programmatically scrape/borrow existing public-domain question lists** or **synthesize bespoke questions via a constrained generative pipeline**.

### 5.1 Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)

We evaluate three competing acquisition methodologies:
* **Hypothesis A (Scrape & Ingest)**: Aggregate public domain JSON repositories, GitHub party decks, and generic web lists.
* **Hypothesis B (Bespoke Constrained Generation)**: Author bespoke prompts using a structured LLM generation pipeline governed by strict syntactic schemas and deterministic validation filters.
* **Hypothesis C (Hybrid Scrape & Rewrite)**: Scrape external public domain datasets as raw idea seeds, then manually rewrite them to meet project constraints.

### 5.2 Comparative Evaluation Matrix

| Evaluation Dimension | Option A: Public Domain Scraping | Option B: Bespoke Programmatic Generation | Option C: Hybrid Curation & Rewrite |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Syntax & Length Compliance** | **0% – Catastrophic Failure**. >95% of public lists start with *"Never have I ever"*, exceed 75 characters, and lack past-participle syntax. | **100% – Deterministic**. Enforced via prompt schemas and post-generation regex filters. | **50% – Labor Intensive**. Requires rewriting every single harvested card manually. |
| **Tonal Modernity & Culture** | **Poor**. Dominated by dated 2010–2016 tropes (Facebook pokes, texting T9, physical slapstick). | **Exceptional**. Captures contemporary digital life (Venmo, BeReal, TikTok, Uber, Hinge, Notes app). | **Moderate**. Selective picking improves tone, but legacy source material anchors thinking to old clichés. |
| **Granular 10-Level Calibration** | **Non-Existent**. Public lists use crude binary tags (*"Clean"*, *"Dirty"* or *"Kids"*, *"Teens"*, *"Adults"*). | **Exact (10/10)**. Prompts are seeded specifically against the exact Level 1–10 temperature rubric. | **Poor**. Difficult to retrofit binary public decks into a 10-level progressive escalation gradient. |
| **Category Taxonomy Adherence** | **0%**. None of the 16 project categories exist in public domain repositories. | **100%**. Every batch is generated specifically for its target category slot. | **Low**. Requires re-tagging and extensive structural shoehorning. |
| **Intellectual Property Safety** | **Moderate Risk**. Public internet lists frequently infringe on copyrighted card games (*Cards Against Humanity*, *Truth or Drink*). | **100% Clean IP**. Synthesized novel prompts with zero direct phrase copying. | **Moderate Risk**. Danger of retaining signature phrasing from commercial proprietary games. |
| **Production Velocity & Cost** | **High Initial Speed / Huge Cleanup Debt**. Fast to fetch; weeks spent filtering and fixing syntax. | **Fast & Highly Scalable**. 400 questions can be synthesized and verified in minutes. | **Extremely Slow**. Requires dozens of hours of manual editorial rewrites. |

### 5.3 Diagnostic Verdict & ICD 203 Recommendation

- **Analytic Verdict**: **Hypothesis B (Bespoke Programmatic Generation)** is unequivocally superior across all six technical dimensions.
- **Justification**: The project’s strict mechanical constraints (`15 <= chars <= 68`, `3 <= words <= 12`, verb-first syntax, no prefix, and exact 16-category taxonomy across 10 levels) make scraping existing internet lists a net-negative efficiency trap. You spend more time stripping bad prefixes, rewriting run-on sentences, and discarding tired clichés than it takes to synthesize tailored prompts from scratch.
