## Executive Summary

While *Never Have I Ever* (NHIE) operates on **retrospective self-incrimination** ("Drink if you did it"), *Most Likely To* (MLT) functions as an **active, kinetic peer-directed indictment engine**. 

In NHIE, players look inward at their personal history and make private, unilateral disclosures. In MLT, players look outward across the circle, make instant social evaluations of their friends' behavioral predispositions, and physically point their fingers on a synchronized countdown. 

When an MLT prompt fails, it fails for one of two systemic failure modes:
1. **The Abstract Cliché Trap**: The prompt is so generic and safe (*"Become a millionaire"*, *"Travel the world"*, *"Get married first"*) that votes scatter randomly. There is no social friction, no character revelation, and zero conversational follow-up.
2. **The "Dave Trap" (Scapegoating Pathology)**: The prompt tests an uncalibrated negative axis (*"End up in jail"*, *"Black out first"*, *"Lose their phone"*). In almost every friend group, one designated "chaotic friend" receives 90% of all votes across multiple rounds. This induces emotional exhaustion, resentment, perceived bullying, and rapid game death.

The **Golden Maxim of Most Likely To Game Design** formulated in this dossier is:
> **"The card is the indictment; the players are the jury; the defense is the entertainment."**
> A high-impact MLT prompt does not describe an accomplished past fact; it paints a vivid, polarized future scenario (4 to 12 words, 15 to 70 characters) that triggers instantaneous recognition, splits the room between competing archetypes, and forces an explosive outcry from the accused: *"Bakit ako?!"* ("Why me?!").

This technical dossier delivers the complete empirical, psychological, sociolinguistic, and mechanical architecture required to author, calibrate, and scale the 500-question MLT deck for *Tagay Ta*, localized for Philippine *barkada* and *inuman* culture.

## 1. Behavioral Game Design: NHIE vs. Most Likely To

To engineer prompts that maximize kinetic engagement, we must contrast the social physics of NHIE and MLT across 8 core behavioral dimensions.

```
+──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
|                                THE SOCIAL PHYSICS COMPARISON                                     |
+────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────+
| DIMENSION                      | NEVER HAVE I EVER (NHIE)       | MOST LIKELY TO (MLT)          |
+────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────+
| 1. Direction of Gaze           | Inward (Self-reflection)       | Outward (Peer surveillance)   |
| 2. Temporal Focus              | Past (Retrospective fact)      | Future / Disposition (Plausible) |
| 3. Primary Social Emotion      | Confessional guilt / relief    | Accusatory shock / vindication |
| 4. Physical Action             | Sip drink / drop finger        | Synchronous finger-point      |
| 5. Vocal Outburst              | "Wait, you did that?!"         | "Bakit ako?! Bakit hindi siya?!"|
| 6. Risk Profile                | Exposure of hidden past        | Confrontation with reputation |
| 7. Minimum Group Size          | 3 players                      | 4 to 10+ players (Scales wide)|
| 8. Catalytic Engine            | Information Gap (The Story)    | Attribution Debate (The Defense) |
+────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────+
```

### 1.1 Social Attribution Theory (Heider, 1958; Kelley, 1967)
In social psychology, Fritz Heider and Harold Kelley demonstrated that humans constantly make causal attributions about others' actions, balancing **internal disposition** (personality traits, moral character) against **external situations** (circumstances, bad luck).
* **NHIE reveals Situations**: *"I stayed out until sunrise because my car broke down."*
* **MLT attacks Dispositions**: *"You would stay out until sunrise because you have zero impulse control and FOMO."*

Because an MLT vote reflects how one's closest peers perceive one's core personality, the stakes are viscerally higher. The card serves as a mirror reflecting the group's collective, unvarnished consensus back at the individual.

### 1.2 The Synchronous Reveal: Why Simultaneous Pointing Creates High-Voltage Energy
Unlike turn-based games where players deliberate sequentially, MLT relies on the **Simultaneous Physical Reveal**.
1. **No Bandwagoning**: Counting down "3-2-1... POINT!" prevents trailing votes. Players cannot wait to see where the majority points before casting their own vote.
2. **The Millisecond Gaze Collision**: At the exact moment of pointing, all eyes dart across the table. When three fingers suddenly converge on one person, the target experiences a sudden spike in adrenaline.
3. **Plausible Deniability Dissolution**: In NHIE, a player can quietly sip without drawing attention. In MLT, the target cannot hide. They are physically spotlighted by multiple outstretched arms.

### 2.1 Concrete Situational Specificity vs. Abstract Clichés
Allan Paivio’s Dual-Coding Theory (Paivio, 1971) proves that concrete situational markers evoke dual cognitive encoding (auditory + visual/sensory). Abstract prompts fail to generate mental imagery, resulting in hesitation and scattered voting.

### 2.2 The Anti-Hero vs. Villain Boundary in Peer Pointing
In NHIE, admitting to a crime harms only the confessor. In MLT, pointing at a peer for an irredeemably toxic trait can inflict genuine relational damage.
* **The Lovable Anti-Hero (Mandatory Sweet Spot)**: Prompts target relatable foibles driven by mild vanity, laziness, petty pride, dramatic flair, or romantic impulsiveness (*marupok*). The room points with laughter and affection.
* **The Malignant Villain (Strictly Banned)**: Prompts targeting cruelty, theft of significant assets, workplace sabotage, infidelity causing divorce, or predatory behavior. Pointing at someone for these acts transforms playful banter into an uncomfortable public trial.

### 3.1 The 2-Second Acoustic Noise Gate
Party environments (bars, condo living rooms, karaoke rooms) operate at 75 to 90 dB of ambient sound. 
* Prompts must be articulated in a **single vocal breath** (under 2.5 seconds).
* Multi-clause subordinations (*"Who is most likely to, while attending an event where they do not know anyone..."*) collapse immediately. Before the reader finishes the clause, the listener has forgotten the beginning.
* Strict syllable constraints prevent tongue-twisting. Every prompt must pass the **Loud-Room Vocal Test**: legible at a glance, instantly shouted without stuttering.

## 4. The Split-Room Controversy Index & Anti-Scapegoat Engineering

### 4.1 Deconstructing "The Dave Trap" (The Scapegoating Pathology)
In standard party card games, naive prompts like *"Most likely to go to jail"* or *"Most likely to pass out first"* suffer from an acute behavioral pathology known as **The Dave Trap**.

In René Girard’s anthropological theory of the Scapegoat (*Le Bouc émissaire*, 1982) and Henri Tajfel’s Social Identity Theory (1979), human groups under minimal cognitive friction naturally converge on an established outlier to discharge social tension. 

```
                               THE DAVE TRAP PATHOLOGY
                               
      ROUND 1                   ROUND 3                   ROUND 5
   "Get arrested"           "Black out first"        "Lose their phone"
   
        [●]                       [●]                       [●]
       ┌─┼─┐                     ┌─┼─┐                     ┌─┼─┐
      ▲  │  ▲                   ▲  │  ▲                   ▲  │  ▲
     ╱   │   ╲                 ╱   │   ╲                 ╱   │   ╲
   [●]  [●]  [●]             [●]  [●]  [●]             [●]  [●]  [●]
     ╲   │   ╱                 ╲   │   ╱                 ╲   │   ╱
      ▼  │  ▼                   ▼  │  ▼                   ▼  │  ▼
        [DAVE]                    [DAVE]                    [DAVE]
      (Laughter)             (Nervous Smile)           (Pikon / Resentment)
      Entropy = 0 bits        Entropy = 0 bits          GAME COLLAPSE
```

If the deck presents questions that align along a single axis of "chaotic vs. responsible", the group will point at "Dave" (the designated wild friend) every single round:
1. **Round 1**: Dave laughs and takes a sip. Fun and harmless.
2. **Round 3**: Dave forces a smile. The rest of the group is coasting on autopilot.
3. **Round 5**: Dave feels emotionally targeted and excluded. He retreats into stony silence (*tampo*) or retaliates with genuine anger (*pikon*). The room freezes. The game dies.

### 4.2 Mathematical Formulation of the Split-Room Controversy Index
To eliminate the Dave Trap, we model vote distributions using Claude Shannon’s Information Entropy (Shannon, 1948). 

Let a party circle contain $N$ players. For any prompt $Q$, let $v_i$ represent the number of votes received by player $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$, where $\sum_{i=1}^N v_i = N$. The probability of player $i$ receiving a vote is $p_i = \frac{v_i}{N}$.

The **Normalized Controversy Index** $C(Q)$ is defined as:
$$C(Q) = \frac{-\sum_{i=1}^N p_i \log_2(p_i)}{\log_2(N)}$$

```
                   NORMALIZED CONTROVERSY INDEX C(Q) SPECTRUM
                   
  C(Q) = 0.00                   C(Q) = 0.70 - 0.95                C(Q) = 1.00
  ┌─────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┐
  │ TOTAL SCAPEGOAT             │ GOLDEN POLARIZATION             │ COMPLETE DIFFUSION          │
  │ All N votes land on Dave.   │ 50/50 split across 2 rivals.    │ Everyone points elsewhere.  │
  │ Zero debate, zero suspense. │ Explosive debate, high energy.  │ No consensus, weak stakes.  │
  │ High risk of bullying.      │ Maximum narrative sparring.     │ Tepid round.                │
  └─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Target Range**: High-impact prompts are engineered to produce $0.65 \le C(Q) \le 0.95$. 
* The prompt must possess **interpretive duality**: two equally flawed but psychologically distinct personalities must both feel guilty of the charge.

## 5. Filipino Barkada Dynamics & The Asar vs. Pikon Boundary

### 5.1 Sikolohiyang Pilipino: Kapwa, Biro, and Tampo
In Virgilio Enriquez’s foundational *Sikolohiyang Pilipino* (Filipino Psychology), social interaction is governed by the core concept of **Kapwa** (shared inner self) and the division between two relational categories:
1. **Ibang-Tao (Outsider)**: Interactions must maintain *Hiya* (social propriety/shame) and *Pakikisama* (polite yielding).
2. **Hindi-Ibang-Tao (Insider / "One of Us")**: Found within established *barkadas*, *tropa*, and long-standing friend groups. Here, *Hiya* is replaced by mutual trust, and **Biro** (teasing / playful roasting) becomes the primary currency of affection and intimacy.

```
                           THE FILIPINO SOCIAL SAFETY GRADIENT
                           
   [BIRO / ASAR]             ◄── THE SWEET SPOT ──►           [PIKON / TAMPO]
   "Laro lang!"           "Ate the last chicken skin"      "Triggered real debt/trauma"
   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►
   Relatable Imperfection      Playful Accusation            Real Relational Damage
   Mutual Roasting & Affection Laughter, Defense, Story      Stony Silence, Withholding, Away
   (INSIDER INTIMACY)          (THE ANTI-HERO UNDER TRIAL)   (SOCIAL COLLAPSE)
```

### 5.2 The Asar vs. Pikon Safety Boundary
In Philippine inuman culture, **Asar** (good-natured ribbing) is celebrated, while becoming **Pikon** (taking offense, losing one's temper) is viewed as a social failure. However, a game that induces *pikunan* ruins the night. 

To maintain psychological safety:
* **The "Laro Lang" Invariant**: Prompts must target **voluntary, self-inflicted comedic foibles** (e.g. eating the pulutan too fast, soft-launching on Instagram, waking up late, being *marupok*).
* **The Red-Line Ban**: Prompts must NEVER target non-voluntary hardships, real economic distress (unemployment, inability to pay rent), physical body shaming, reproductive issues, or unresolved family traumas.

## 6. Mechanical & Syntactic Invariants for Tagay Ta MLT

### 6.1 Syntactic Anchor Formulation
In NHIE, every prompt completes the unspoken clause:
`[Never have I ever...] + {Past Participle Verb Phrase}`

In *Most Likely To*, every prompt completes the unspoken spoken call:
* English: `[Who is most likely to...] + {Base Verb / Bare Infinitive Predicate}`
* Taglish / CUPE: `[Sino ang pinaka-posibleng...] + {Base Verb / Bare Infinitive Predicate}`

#### The On-Card Prefix Omission Rule
Cards must **NEVER** print the literal prefix *"Who is most likely to"* or *"Most likely to"*. 
* Omitting the prefix saves **15 to 22 characters** of screen real estate.
* Eliminates vocal stuttering; the reading player already knows the game mode and announces the count.
* Enables responsive, large-type typography on mobile displays without unwanted line-wrapping.

### 7.1 Tie-Breaker Protocols: The Sudden-Death Runoff
When two players receive an identical number of votes:
1. **The Duel Clink (Fast Casual)**: Both tied players take a sip together and exchange playful toasts.
2. **The Sudden-Death Runoff (Competitive)**: The table immediately holds a 3-second revote where players may ONLY point at Player A or Player B. The loser of the runoff takes the drink.

### 7.2 The "Right of Defense" (The 10-Second Rebuttal Plea)
The moment the fingers land, the target with the most votes has the **Right of Defense**:
* The target receives an uninterrupted **10-second rebuttal timer**.
* They may explain their innocence or redirect blame by citing concrete past receipts from another player (*"Bakit ako?! Last week si Mark ang nag-uwi ng pulutan sa Tupperware!"*).
* If the defense makes the room erupt in laughter, the table can hold a quick show of hands to **Transfer the Tagay** to the newly exposed culprit!

## 9. Digital UX Recommendations & Table Facilitation

A mobile party game application should act as a **subtle table facilitator**, not an attention-sucking screen hog. In live play, eyes must remain on friends' faces, not glued to phone displays.

```
                               MOBILE COMPANION SCREEN
                               
              ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │ [LEVEL 5]  PARTY TIER              [TIE] [⚙]  │
              │                                               │
              │              DISAPPEAR INTO                   │
              │              THIN AIR WHEN                    │
              │              THE BILL ARRIVES.                │
              │                                               │
              │  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
              │  │  (( 3... 2... 1... POINT! ))            │  │  <-- Pulsing Haptic Engine
              │  └─────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
              │                                               │
              │  [ ⏱ 10s DEFENSE ]      [ ⚔ TIE-BREAKER ]     │  <-- Action Accelerators
              └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 9.1 Minimalist Table Facilitation Features
1. **The Synchronized 3-2-1 Audio/Haptic Countdown Engine**:
   - Tapping the card screen triggers a distinct, 3-second audio-haptic cadence: *Tick... Tick... BEEP!*
   - Synchronizes finger-pointing across loud rooms so no player can stall or cheat.
2. **The 10-Second Defense Rebuttal Timer**:
   - A one-tap button starting a ticking 10-second timer with a humorous buzzer.
   - Enforces the "Right of Defense" without allowing arguments to drag into long filibusters.
3. **The Tie-Breaker Duel Button**:
   - If the room splits 50/50, tapping "Tie-Breaker" triggers a rapid visual duel flip, selecting between candidates or displaying: *"BOTH DRINK!"*
4. **Unanimous Flare Animation**:
   - Tapping "Unanimous!" triggers a red screen flare: *"FIRING SQUAD: DOUBLE SHOT!"*
5. **No Screen-Pointing Required**:
   - The digital app should NOT require players to input their votes on screen. In-person physical finger-pointing is 100x faster, louder, and more visceral than tapping UI avatars.
